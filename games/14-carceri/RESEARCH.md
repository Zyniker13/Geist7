# 14 Carceri — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier NONE. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Giovanni Battista Piranesi, Carceri d'invenzione (Imaginary Prisons):** first state c. 1749–50 (14 plates); second state 1761 (16 plates, reworked darker). Metropolitan Museum holds impressions (Verified for c. 1749–50 objects). Public-domain images available via the Met Open Access program.
- **M. C. Escher** (Relativity, 1953; Ascending and Descending, 1960) and the **Penrose stairs** (Lionel and Roger Penrose, 1958) for impossible geometry. Escher works are under copyright in many jurisdictions; reference only.
- **Palette:** iron-gall and etching ink black, warm paper `#EFE6D3`, plate tone grey, a single accent (lamp ochre) for interactive elements.
- **Typography:** an 18th-century Roman (Caslon or Bodoni-adjacent) for titles; plate numbers in Roman numerals (as on Piranesi's plates).
- **Materials:** etched copper plate (line density shading), deckle-edged paper, foxing.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Graph-puzzle and stealth games:** Hitman GO and Lara Croft GO (turn-based node graphs with telegraphed guards), Monument Valley (impossible geometry), The Lost Vikings (three characters with distinct skills).
- **Design theory:** telegraphed deterministic enemies make stealth a puzzle (INV-1).

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Piranesi (1720–1778), Venetian-born engraver in Rome; the Carceri influenced Romanticism (De Quincey, Coleridge). Secondary.
- Sensitivities: prisons and incarceration; avoid torture imagery (some plates show instruments); keep prisoners' escape heroic and non-violent.
