# 20 The Sum of Small Wars — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL (Kōnane, Mū tōrere). Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Josef Albers, Interaction of Color (1963)** and the Homage to the Square series (Josef and Anni Albers Foundation). Colour fields per region. Secondary.
- **Kōnane boards:** stone papamū at Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau National Historical Park (Hawaiʻi); Bishop Museum collections. Verified (NPS rules sheet; Bishop Museum release).
- **Mū tōrere:** associated with Ngāti Porou (East Coast, North Island); boards drawn on the ground, on wood, or on flax mats. Secondary (Te Ara).
- **Palette:** Albers-like flat fields; Kōnane region uses lava black and coral white; Mū tōrere region uses earth and harakeke (flax) tones, both only after advisor review.
- **Typography:** geometric sans with full support for ʻokina (U+02BB) and macrons (ā, ē, ī, ō, ū).
- **Materials:** stone, lava, coral, carved wood, paper colour swatches.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Combinatorial game theory:** Berlekamp, Conway, and Guy, Winning Ways (2nd ed., 2001–2004); Conway, On Numbers and Games (1976); Siegel, Combinatorial Game Theory (2013); CGSuite software.
- **Kōnane analysis:** Ernst (1995) computed values of small Kōnane positions. Verified.
- **Mū tōrere analysis:** Straffin (1995) analysed the position graph. Verified.
- **Design note:** sums make values playable; the hottest-region heuristic is teachable.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- **Kōnane** is a traditional Hawaiian strategy game; revived through tournaments (Bishop Museum hosted the first statewide tournament in modern history). Living practice. Verified.
- **Mū tōrere** is a Māori game associated with Ngāti Porou; whether it predates European contact is discussed in sources. Secondary (Te Ara).
- Sacred vs secular: both are secular games; carved boards may carry iwi-specific designs; avoid copying carvings (whakairo) or tā moko patterns.
- Avoid: "primitive math" framing; misrendered diacritics.
