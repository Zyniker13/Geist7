# 05 Watered Stock — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier NONE (fictional map and companies). Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Security engraving:** 19th-century bond and stock certificates by the American Bank Note Company and Bradbury Wilkinson; guilloche lathe patterns (rose engine). Museum of American Finance and Smithsonian National Postal Museum (banknote and stamp engraving) collections are good study sources.
- **Railway cartography:** 1880s–1900s railway route maps (Library of Congress "Railroad Maps, 1828–1900" collection).
- **Palette:** certificate paper, banknote green, engraving ink, gold leaf; corporation colours are muted inks so they print-read on paper.
- **Typography:** engraved roman and Clarendon/Egyptian slab faces typical of period certificates; numerals in old-style for prices.
- **Materials:** cotton rag paper, intaglio ink relief, embossed seals, perforations.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **1830: Railways and Robber Barons** (Francis Tresham, 1986) established the 18xx core: private auction, stock rounds, operating rounds, train rusting, presidency.
- **1846: The Race for the Midwest** (Thomas Lehmann) for shorter play and different private handling; **18Chesapeake** (Scott Petersen) as an entry 18xx.
- **18xx.games** demonstrates async web play and automatic route calculation; use for UX patterns only.
- **Design note:** 18xx players expect exact rules; ADR-0003 must list every deviation. The "watered stock" title refers to the historical practice of issuing shares beyond asset value.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Gilded Age railroad finance: stock watering, pools, and receiverships (e.g., the Erie War). Keep companies fictional to avoid implying real conduct.
- Sensitivities: avoid depicting railroad construction labour stereotypically (Chinese and Irish workers on the Pacific Railroad); avoid Indigenous land imagery without context. The fictional Ferrous Coast has no named peoples.
