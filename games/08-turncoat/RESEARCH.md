# 08 Turncoat — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier HISTORICAL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Paolo Uccello, The Battle of San Romano** (c. 1438–40): three panels — National Gallery London (NG583), Uffizi (Florence), and the Louvre. Verified for NG583. Use its foreshortened lances, heraldic horse trappings, and orange groves as mood.
- **Heraldry and liveries:** Florentine, Sienese, Milanese, and Venetian civic heraldry; Visconti biscione (Milan) and Florentine giglio. Use simplified original liveries; label any uncertain colours Invented.
- **Funerary monuments:** Uccello's fresco of John Hawkwood (Florence Cathedral, 1436) and Andrea del Castagno's Niccolò da Tolentino (1456). Secondary.
- **Palette:** tempera on gesso, gold leaf, vermilion, azurite, terre verte.
- **Typography:** humanist minuscule-inspired serif (e.g., a Jenson-derived face) for UI; rotunda only for titles.
- **Materials:** gilded gesso, painted wood panels, parchment contracts with wax seals.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Shogi:** drops (captured pieces return as captor's), promotion zone (last three ranks), uchifuzume (no pawn-drop mate), nifu (no two unpromoted pawns on a file). Verified via standard rules (Wikipedia "Shogi").
- **Xiangqi:** the cannon's screen capture inspires the Bombard. Secondary.
- **Chess variants** and fairy pieces (Gilman's "World Game Review" tradition) for movement notation.
- **Design theory:** drops make material convertible; loyalty dampens snowballing by returning some captures.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- **Condottieri** were mercenary captains hired by Italian city-states under a contract (condotta) from the 14th to 16th centuries. Machiavelli criticised them in The Prince (ch. 12). Secondary (Mallett 1974; Caferro 2006).
- Sensitivities: limited; avoid glorifying sack and rape of towns (historical reality); keep violence abstract. Avoid using the papal tiara or crucifixes as factional icons.
