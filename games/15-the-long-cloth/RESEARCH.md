# 15 The Long Cloth — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier HISTORICAL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Bayeux Tapestry** (embroidered linen, c. 1070s; about 68.4 m long, about 50 cm high), Bayeux Museum, Normandy; inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register (2007). Secondary (Wikipedia; museum site not opened).
- **Technique:** wool embroidery on linen; stem stitch (outline and lettering) and laid-and-couched work (fill). Eight to ten wool colours, chiefly terracotta, blue-green, yellow, sage, and dark blue. Secondary.
- **Borders:** upper and lower registers with beasts, Aesop's fables, and (near the end) the fallen and plunderers. Secondary.
- **Palette:** natural linen `#E8DFC8`, madder terracotta `#A5523A`, woad blue `#3C5A73`, weld yellow `#C9A43A`, sage `#7F8B5A`, dark blue-black.
- **Typography:** inscriptional capitals modelled on the Tapestry's lettering for captions (original font), a quiet serif for UI.
- **Materials:** linen weave, wool thread with visible twist, couching stitches.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Card-driven wargames:** Hannibal: Rome vs Carthage, Commands & Colors (Richard Borg; card-activated sections), and Battle Line/Schotten Totten (lane control).
- **Linear battlefields:** Ancient tactics games on strips; the 1D board keeps iPhone play legible.
- **Design note:** the border mechanic turns narrative margins into a game layer.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Narrative of events leading to the Norman Conquest and the Battle of Hastings (14 October 1066). Likely commissioned by Bishop Odo of Bayeux and probably made in England (Canterbury). Secondary (Hicks 2006).
- Sensitivities: violence shown in the original borders (mutilated dead); keep abstract. Avoid Anglo-Saxon or Norman nationalism and modern far-right uses of medieval imagery.
- Debates: Harold's death (arrow in the eye vs. cut down) and the feigned retreats are debated; label Reconstructed.
