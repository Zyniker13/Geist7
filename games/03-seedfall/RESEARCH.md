# 03 Seedfall — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Boards:** carved Bao boards from Zanzibar and the Swahili coast; Oware boards from Ghana (Asante) with carved stands; Pallanguzhi boards (brass or wood, 14 pits) from Tamil Nadu; Congkak boats-shaped boards (Malaysia). Penn Museum, British Museum, and National Museum of African Art collections. Secondary.
- **Palette:** dark hardwood, brass, cowrie white, molucca nut grey (oware seeds), red earth.
- **Typography:** clean humanist sans with Swahili, Twi, Tamil, and Malay glossary support.
- **Materials:** carved wood, seeds (molucca nuts), cowries, tamarind seeds.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Oware (abapa):** 2 × 6 pits, sowing anticlockwise, captures of 2 or 3; grand-slam rule variants (forbidden, allowed without capture, etc.). Verified (Wikipedia).
- **Bao la Kiswahili:** 4 × 8 board; mtaji (capturing) and takasa (non-capturing) turns (Secondary); nyumba (house) and kichwa (end pits) (Verified, Game Cabinet; Wikipedia).
- **Pallanguzhi:** 2 × 7 pits (14). Verified (India Board Games Archive).
- **Congkak:** kampung (village pits) and rumah (house/store). Secondary.
- **Analysis:** Oware (Awari) solved by Romein and Bal (2002) as a draw. Secondary.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Mancala games are played across Africa, the Middle East, South and Southeast Asia, and the Caribbean; Bao is a central social game in Zanzibar with recognised masters (bingwa). Secondary (de Voogt).
- Sensitivities: avoid "African game" monolith framing; name each tradition; avoid religious inscriptions on carved doors; no caricatures.

## 6. Audio and Music Direction
- Regional palettes per tradition (taarab strings for Bao, highlife guitar for Oware, nadaswaram/veena idioms for Pallanguzhi, gamelan-adjacent percussion for Congkak), each original and credited; seed clacks as the core sound.
