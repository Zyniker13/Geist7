# 07 Rosette — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier HISTORICAL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Objects:** British Museum Royal Game of Ur board (from the Royal Cemetery at Ur, excavated by Leonard Woolley, c. 2600–2400 BCE): shell, red limestone, and lapis lazuli inlay. Penn Museum holds further Ur boards and finds from the same excavation.
- **The tablet:** BM 33333B, cuneiform tablet by the scribe Itti-Marduk-balāṭu (177–176 BCE), in the British Museum.
- **Palette:** lapis `#26428B`, shell `#EFE6D2`, red limestone `#A0422D`, bitumen `#2B2620`, gold `#C9A44C`.
- **Typography:** a clean humanist sans (Avenir Next) for UI; cuneiform only as decorative texture from public-domain tablet photographs, never as fake text.
- **Materials:** shell inlay, lapis chips, bitumen bedding, worn wood.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Royal Game of Ur:** the modern ruleset (7 pieces, 4 tetrahedral dice, rosettes give extra roll and safety) is a reconstruction combining excavated equipment with Finkel's reading of the later tablet. RoyalUr.net has solved it and publishes analysis. Verified.
- **The tablet as attested:** five pieces per player and astragal dice; squares carry fortunes. Verified (Wikipedia summary of Finkel 2007; British Museum bibliographic record).
- **Doubling cube:** from backgammon (introduced in 1920s New York). Secondary.
- **Senet:** many reconstructions (Kendall, Bell); none authoritative. **Patolli:** Aztec race game described by Diego Durán (16th century). Secondary.
- **Design note:** race games are luck-heavy; the wager layer gives skill expression (as in backgammon).

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Mesopotamian game played for over two millennia across the Near East. No living tradition except reported Kochi (India) survival of a related game (Secondary; Finkel mentions it); do not claim continuity.
- Sensitivities: Iraqi heritage, looted antiquities, and museum provenance; avoid depicting royal-tomb human sacrifice (the Ur "death pits") as flavour. Avoid gambling glamour aimed at minors.

## 6. Audio and Music Direction
- Original music using lyre-like plucked strings (the Lyres of Ur are famous), frame drums, and reed pipes; no claim of authentic Sumerian music. Tavern ambience with dice on wood.
