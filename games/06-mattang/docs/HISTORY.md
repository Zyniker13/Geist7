# Mattang — History

Labels used on every in-game historical note (HIST3.1): **Attested** (in the sources), **Reconstructed** (built from a stated basis), **Invented** (made for play).

## Attested (with source)

- Marshallese navigators read ocean swells — including their reflection and refraction around islands — to find land; the knowledge is taught on land and held as a protected tradition. → Genz et al. 2009 (C-1, C-2).
- Stick charts (mattang, meddo, rebbelib, wapepe) were teaching tools made of pandanus ribs, coconut fibre, and shells representing swells and islands; charts were memorised, not carried at sea. → Genz et al. 2009; Ascher 1995; Smithsonian collections notes (C-1, C-3).
- The glossary terms booj ("knot", the meeting of swells), okar ("root", a line of nodes), dilep ("backbone", the wave path between islands), and kaelep (western swell) describe real swell phenomena. → Genz et al. 2009; Genz 2011. (Spellings pending advisor confirmation; SOURCING §4.)
- ri-meto ("person of the ocean") is a chiefly-conferred title, never a game rank. → SOURCING §3 (restricted).

## Reconstructed (with the reconstruction's basis)

- The discrete swell field: swells entering from fixed sides as straight rays that reflect off island faces by `(2n − d + 3) mod 6`, refract once beside an island, and cross to form nodes ("booj") whose strength counts distinct swells. **Basis:** the swell-interaction descriptions in Genz et al. 2009, made discrete and deterministic for play, following Ascher's formal stick-chart analysis (Ising 1998). The numeric rules (R-12–R-14, node chains, becalming) are the game's reconstruction of the described phenomena, not claims about actual physics.
- The generic chart-frame UI (ribs, shells, frame) that echoes stick-chart construction without copying any specific chart. **Basis:** the construction materials and structure described in Ascher 1995 and Smithsonian notes.

## Invented (for play)

- The scoring game entirely: hex ocean of radius 7, home atolls, the shared island pool, canoes, launches, sailing with node chains, towing becalmed canoes, landing at the rival atoll, the 3-landings victory, tie-breaks (R-1–R-11, R-17–R-23).
- AI tiers (Paddler, Sailor, Navigator, Deep Water), voyages, daily charts, puzzles, achievements.
- All titles and text framing the game as a game: Mattang is an abstract strategy game about reading swells, not a navigation trainer.
