# 18 Hanmoto — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Ukiyo-e masters (study only):** Katsushika Hokusai, Utagawa Hiroshige, Kitagawa Utamaro, Tōshūsai Sharaku, Tsukioka Yoshitoshi. Collections: Tokyo National Museum, Museum of Fine Arts Boston (largest outside Japan), British Museum, Art Institute of Chicago, Fitzwilliam Museum (Yoshitoshi), Library of Congress.
- **Process references:** Adachi Institute of Woodblock Prints (Tokyo); Asian Art Museum (San Francisco) process guide. Verified (Asian Art Museum).
- **Palette (Edo pigments):** sumi black, beni (safflower red), ai (indigo), bero/bero-ai (Prussian blue, from c. 1829 in landscape prints), kihada yellow, gofun white, tan (red lead). Secondary.
- **Typography:** clean serif/sans for UI; Japanese text only from reviewed sources; no faux-Asian Latin fonts.
- **Materials:** washi (kōzo paper), cherry-wood blocks (yamazakura), baren, carving knives, embossing (karazuri), mica ground (kirazuri).

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Worker placement:** Agricola, Lords of Waterdeep; **production chains:** Puerto Rico; **fashion markets:** Concordia.
- **Historical system:** publishers (hanmoto) financed and coordinated artist, carver, and printer; censorship seals (aratame, kiwame) appear on prints. Verified (Wikipedia glossary; University of Oregon JSMA glossary).

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Edo period (1603–1868) urban popular culture; the Kansei (1787–1793) and Tenpō (1841–1843) reforms restricted luxury and certain subjects (actor and courtesan prints). Secondary.
- Sacred vs secular: ukiyo-e is secular; avoid Buddhist and Shinto imagery as commodities. Exclude shunga (erotic prints).
- Living communities: contemporary woodblock artisans (Adachi Foundation; Takezasado in Kyoto), print collectors' societies.
- Avoid: geisha and samurai clichés, Orientalist framing, fake Japanese characters.

## 6. Audio and Music Direction
- Shamisen and shakuhachi idioms recorded by trained players (original compositions), Edo street ambience (vendors, geta), carving and rubbing foley (baren on paper). Avoid temple bells and religious chant.
