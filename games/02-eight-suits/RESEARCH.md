# 02 Eight Suits — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Ganjifa cards:** round hand-painted cards (lacquered cloth or paper) from Mughal India; regional schools in Sawantwadi (Maharashtra), Odisha, Mysuru, and Bishnupur (West Bengal). Verified (IGNCA; Handicrafts India).
- **Collections:** Ashmolean Museum (Oxford) Persian and Indian playing cards; V&A; British Museum; Victoria Memorial Hall. Verified (Ashmolean blog).
- **Palette:** lacquer red, gold, black, white, and suit-specific colours from the Mughal pattern.
- **Typography:** a refined serif; Persian/Urdu suit names in Nastaliq only from reviewed text.
- **Materials:** lacquered round cards, tamarind-seed paste board (Sawantwadi), hand-painted brushwork.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Mughal Ganjifa:** 96 cards in 8 suits of 12 (2 courts — Mir/Shah and Wazir — and 10 numerals); suits: Taj (crown), Safed (white), Shamsher (sword), Ghulam (slave/servant), Chang (harp), Surkh (red), Barat (document), Qimash (goods). Verified (IGNCA; IPCS pattern sheet).
- **Dashavatara Ganjifa:** 120 cards in 10 suits (Vishnu's avatars) — excluded. Verified.
- **Trick-taking games:** Hearts, Spades, and the traditional Ganjifa game ("hamrang"-style trick play; Secondary).
- **Design note:** tricks drive actions (Invented).

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Ganjifa came to India via Persia and flourished under the Mughals (16th century onward); later Hindu decks (Dashavatara) emerged. Secondary.
- Living craft: Sawantwadi lacquer artisans (Chitari community) keep Ganjifa alive. Verified (Handicrafts India).
- Sensitivities: no deity imagery; no Quranic text; no caricatured rulers; avoid "exotic Orient" tropes.
