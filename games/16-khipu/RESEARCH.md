# 16 Khipu — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Khipu collections:** Museo Nacional de Arqueología, Antropología e Historia del Perú (Lima); Museo Larco (Lima); Ethnologisches Museum Berlin (large khipu holding); American Museum of Natural History (Locke's khipu); Harvard's Open Khipu Repository (data) and the former Khipu Database Project. Secondary.
- **Smithsonian NMAI "The Great Inka Road: Engineering an Empire"** exhibition and its educational glossary. Verified (glossary PDF).
- **Textiles:** Andean tocapu (geometric squares) and cumbi cloth; use only generic step-fret until advisors approve tocapu-like motifs.
- **Landscape:** Moray terraces, Pisac, qullqa at Huánuco Pampa and Cotapachi.
- **Palette:** natural camelid wool browns and creams, cochineal red, indigo, qolle yellow, terrace green, puna straw.
- **Typography:** a sturdy humanist sans; Quechua uses Latin letters with apostrophes for ejectives (ch', k', p', q', t') and ñ; verify glyphs.
- **Materials:** spun cotton and camelid fibre cords, knot relief, adobe, fieldstone, ichu grass thatch.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Logistics sims:** Banished, Anno, and Frostpunk for storage and spoilage; Papers, Please and Return of the Obra Dinn for audit-and-discrepancy reasoning.
- **Khipu numeracy:** base-10 positional knots (Locke 1923; Ascher and Ascher, "Code of the Quipu", 1981). Secondary.
- **Decipherment:** Medrano and Urton (2018) matched a set of Santa Valley khipus to Spanish census records. Verified.
- **Vertical archipelago:** Murra's model of Andean multi-zone resource access. Secondary.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- The Inka state (Tawantinsuyu, 15th–16th century) used khipu for accounting and possibly narrative; khipu-making continued in some communities into recent times (e.g., San Andrés de Tupicocha keeps patrimonial khipu). Secondary (Salomon, "The Cord Keepers", 2004).
- Sacred vs secular: huacas (sacred places and objects), apus (mountain beings), mallqui (ancestor mummies), and coca in ritual are sacred; never resources.
- Living communities: Quechua-speaking peoples of Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Argentina, and Chile; Aymara communities in the altiplano.
- Avoid: conquest narratives and "lost civilisation" framing; extractive "El Dorado" tropes; presenting mit'a as benevolent (it was a labour obligation; colonial mita was brutal and distinct).

## 6. Audio and Music Direction
- Andean instruments (quena, siku panpipes, charango, bombo) recorded by Andean musicians under licence; avoid sacred ritual music (e.g., offerings to apus). Wind on the puna, llama bells, cord rustle for reports.
