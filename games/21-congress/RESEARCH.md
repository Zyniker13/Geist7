# 21 Congress — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier NONE (fictional powers). Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **James Gillray** (1756–1815) and **Isaac Cruikshank** political caricature; Gillray's "The Plumb-pudding in danger" (1805) — Pitt and Napoleon carving the globe — is the tonal reference for the map-carving theme. Secondary.
- **Jean-Baptiste Isabey, "The Congress of Vienna"** (1815 drawing/engraving) for composition of delegates. Secondary.
- **Palette:** hand-coloured etching washes (watercolour over line): madder, Prussian blue, gamboge, sap green on cream paper.
- **Typography:** Didone (Bodoni/Didot) for titles; a Scotch Roman for body; copperplate script for signatures.
- **Materials:** laid paper, wax seals with ribbons, red tape, quill marks.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Diplomacy** (Allan B. Calhamer, 1959): negotiation with simultaneous orders; **Cosmic Encounter** for deal-making; **Article 27: The UN Security Council Game** for timed negotiation with vetoes; **Quo Vadis?** (Knizia) for votes and deals.
- **AI diplomacy research:** Meta's CICERO (2022) played no-press-plus-dialogue Diplomacy; our structured offers avoid natural-language generation.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- Congress of Vienna (1814–1815) redrew Europe after the Napoleonic Wars; also Congress of Berlin (1878) and Paris (1919). The game is fictional to avoid modern national sensitivities (e.g., partition of Poland, Balkan borders).
