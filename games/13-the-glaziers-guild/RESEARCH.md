# 13 The Glaziers' Guild — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier HISTORICAL. Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Chartres Cathedral** (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 1979): 12th–13th-century glass, the famous "Chartres blue". Secondary.
- **Corpus Vitrearum Medii Aevi (CVMA):** international project cataloguing medieval stained glass; CVMA Great Britain hosts an online picture archive. Secondary (site not opened this pass).
- **Other references:** Sainte-Chapelle (Paris); Canterbury Cathedral; grisaille windows of the Cistercians (abstract foliate patterns — closest to the game's abstract windows); V&A stained-glass collection.
- **Palette (pot-metal glass):** ruby (copper red, flashed), cobalt blue, emerald, gold/silver-stain yellow (silver stain from c. 1300), murrey (purple), white (greenish clear).
- **Typography:** Lombardic capitals for titles; textura only for headings; a readable serif for body.
- **Materials:** pot-metal glass with seeds and bubbles, lead cames (H-section), saddle bars, iron armatures, vitreous paint.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Auction games:** Modern Art (Reiner Knizia; open, once-around, sealed, fixed-price, and double auctions), Ra, and For Sale.
- **Polyomino placement:** Patchwork, Cottage Garden, and Tetris-like spatial puzzles.
- **Design note:** three formats teach different auction instincts; the hinge rule creates spatial tension unique to glass.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- **Theophilus Presbyter, De diversis artibus** (c. 1100–1120): Book II describes glassmaking and window construction (cutting with a hot iron, grozing, leading). Secondary.
- **Guilds:** medieval glaziers' guilds (e.g., Worshipful Company of Glaziers, London, records from 14th century). Secondary.
- Sensitivities: stained glass is mostly in churches; keep windows abstract and avoid saints, Christ, and biblical scenes as game objects.

## 6. Audio and Music Direction
- Plainchant-adjacent sounds are sacred; prefer secular medieval instruments (vielle, gittern, recorder) in original pieces; glass clinks, came bending, workshop fire, auction gavel (wooden mallet).
