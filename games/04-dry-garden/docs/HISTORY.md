# Dry Garden — History

Labels used on every in-game historical note (HIST3.1): **Attested** (in the
sources), **Reconstructed** (built from a stated basis), **Invented** (made for
play).

## Attested (with source)

- Dry landscape gardens (karesansui) developed in medieval Japan and are
  strongly associated with Zen temples from the Muromachi period onward.
  → `RESEARCH.md` §3; NAJGA "The Dry Landscape Garden" (C-1).
- The Sakuteiki (作庭記), an eleventh-century garden manual, is the oldest
  known Japanese garden manual. → `RESEARCH.md` §3; NAJGA (C-2).
- Asymmetric stone composition and odd groupings are widely taught Japanese
  garden design principles; the seven common raking patterns (samon) are part
  of the living practice. → NAJGA "7 Common Raking Patterns"; NAJGA
  "Garden Rocks" (C-3).
- The Sakuteiki vocabulary omo-ishi, waki-ishi, mae-ishi, and
  sanzon-ishigumi describes stone-arrangement roles. → NAJGA "Garden Rocks"
  (research §1, Verified).

## Reconstructed (with the reconstruction's basis)

- The discrete influence field: each stone radiates rings of decreasing
  influence on a hex lattice; a cell belongs to the player with strictly
  greater total influence. **Basis:** the influence-as-territory reading of
  Go (research §2), made discrete and deterministic for play. The numeric
  curves (R-9) are the game's reconstruction, not a claim about garden design
  practice.
- The rake lines as influence carriers anchored to a stone. **Basis:** the
  raking practice (patterns and their directional grooves) described by NAJGA,
  abstracted into a game action (R-5, R-10).
- The generic courtyard outlines (masks). **Basis:** the general composition
  of walled karesansui courtyards (wall, gravel yard, moss patches, basin),
  deliberately irregular and matched to no specific real garden (avoid list,
  research §3).

## Invented (for play)

- The scoring game entirely: stone sizes and supplies, facing and the viewing
  face bonus, group bonuses for odd-sized groups, the symmetry and collinearity
  laws as hard legality rules, seasons, puzzles, the ladder, daily gardens,
  achievements (R-1–R-15, A.1 additions).
- AI tiers (Novice, Gardener, Master, Ancient Gardener), stats, async matches.
- All titles and text framing the game as a game: Dry Garden is an abstract
  strategy game about setting stones and raking gravel, not a garden-design
  trainer.
