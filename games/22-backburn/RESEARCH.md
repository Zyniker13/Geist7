# 22 Backburn — Art, Design, and Culture Research

Research pass: 15 Nov 2026. Sourcing tier FULL (Indigenous fire stewardship). Status legend: Verified, Secondary, Unverified.

## 1. Art Direction References
- **Topographic and fire-behaviour maps:** USGS topographic sheets; US Forest Service fire progression maps; Australian state fire agency maps. Secondary.
- **Painting references for mood (not style copying):** Australian landscape painting (e.g., Arthur Streeton's "Fire's On", 1891, Art Gallery of New South Wales). Contemporary Aboriginal art must not be imitated.
- **Palette:** eucalypt grey-green, dry grass straw, char black, ember orange `#FF7A1A`, retardant pink-red, smoke violet-grey.
- **Typography:** a utilitarian sans (DIN-like) for map labels and orders.
- **Materials:** contour linework, ash textures, heat shimmer shaders.

## 2. Design Lineage
- **Simulations:** Rothermel surface fire spread model (1972); FARSITE and FlamMap (US Forest Service) as professional relatives; Firewatch and Frostpunk for tone; Into the Breach and Mini Metro for readable command UIs.
- **Fuel models:** Scott and Burgan (2005) 40 standard fuel models. Verified.

## 3. Cultural and Historical Context
- **Indigenous fire stewardship:** cultural burning by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in Australia and by Native American nations in the US; cool, patchy burns create mosaics. Firesticks Alliance (Indigenous-led) and the Indigenous Peoples Burning Network (US, The Nature Conservancy and partners) support practitioners. Verified.
- **Victor Steffensen** (Tagalaka), co-founder of Firesticks, author of "Fire Country" (2020). Verified (Firesticks).
- Sacred vs secular: cultural burning is tied to Country, law, and knowledge held by elders; do not depict ceremonies, sacred sites, or secret knowledge.
- Avoid: framing cultural burning as a technique the player commands; "noble savage" framing; disaster spectacle of real fires (Black Summer 2019–20, Camp Fire 2018).
