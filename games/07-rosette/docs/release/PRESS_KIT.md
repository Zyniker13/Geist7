# Rosette — Press Kit

## Fact sheet

- **Title:** Rosette
- **Genre:** Board / strategy (race game)
- **Platform:** iOS, iPadOS, macOS (iPad-first design)
- **Players:** 1 (vs AI), 2 (pass-and-play), async bot opponents
- **Release:** version 1.0 (date: maintainer, issue #11)
- **Price:** premium, universal purchase (maintainer decision, issue #11)
- **Rating:** 12+ (simulated gambling; no real-money wagering)
- **Languages:** English (1.0)

## One-liner

The Royal Game of Ur played in a smoky tavern circuit, where the dice are
fair but the stakes are not.

## Short pitch (≤ 50 words)

Race seven pieces home on a 4,500-year-old board with four tetrahedral dice,
rosette squares, and sends-home captures. Set a stake, double the cube, play
one-shot omens, and climb a tavern circuit from Ur to Thebes to Tenochtitlan
against opponents with personalities.

## Feature list

- **Faithful core:** the modern Ur ruleset reconstructed from the excavated
  sets — 7 pieces, 20 squares, 4 binary tetrahedra; probabilities 1/16, 4/16,
  6/16, 4/16, 1/16.
- **Two rulesets:** Reconstructed (default) and Tablet (five pieces, astragal
  dice, fortunes on squares — attested where attested, reconstructed where
  gaps are filled), each rule labeled in-game.
- **The wager layer:** per-match stakes, the doubling cube, and omen tokens
  (one-shot rule bends bought between matches).
- **The circuit:** a campaign across historical variants — Finkel's tablet
  rules, the Masters' long route, a Senet-like race (Reconstructed), and
  Patolli on a 52-space cross — ending in the final tavern's tournament.
- **Opponents with personalities:** careful to reckless bots that strengthen
  deeper into the circuit; async decisions keep the board at 60 fps.
- **Local pass-and-play** on one device; async online matches after launch.
- **Telemetry-free:** no accounts for the core game, no analytics, and an
  on-device stats export you keep.

## Historical note

The Royal Game of Ur's rules survive only partly: the Babylonian tablet of
Itti-Marduk-balāṭu (BM 33333B, 177–176 BCE) describes five pieces per player
and astragal dice, while the 7-piece, tetrahedral-dice ruleset is a modern
reconstruction popularised by Irving Finkel and RoyalUr.net. Rosette ships
both and labels every rule Reconstructed or Attested (sources:
`docs/HISTORY.md`).
